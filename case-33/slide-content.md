### Slide 4 — The goods: what a neural computer is and which rules apply (Student A)

**On the slide**

- **Nature:** a computer whose hardware imitates neurons. It changes the weights and the number of connections between its computing elements based on earlier data.
- **Why dual-use:** the same pattern recognition serves speech and image analysis, and also sonar, radar and target recognition.
- **Control entry:** 4A004.b — EU dual-use list, Annex I to Regulation (EU) 2021/821; mirrored in Norway's List II.
- **Tariff code:** HS 8471.50. Norwegian Customs Tariff applies, not TARIC.

| Export from Norway | Import into Mongolia |
|---|---|
| Individual export licence from DEKSA before shipment | No sanctions or embargo against Mongolia |
| End-user statement signed by the buyer | Import declaration at Ulaanbaatar airport customs |
| Export declaration to Norwegian Customs | IT equipment listed as zero-rated (general duty 5 %) |
| No re-export without Norwegian consent | 10 % import VAT, about EUR 58,000 |

**Say (45 s):** "A neural computer is not an ordinary server. Its hardware adjusts its own connections as it learns, like neurons do. That is useful for speech recognition, but equally for sonar or target recognition, so it is a dual-use item under entry 4A004.b. Norway is outside the EU, so it uses its own control list, which mirrors the EU one, and its own customs tariff instead of TARIC. The seller cannot ship without an individual licence from DEKSA, the Norwegian export control directorate, and DEKSA wants a signed end-user statement. Mongolia itself is not sanctioned. Import is simple: a declaration and ten percent VAT."

**Backup for questions:** the EU definition reads "computational devices designed or modified to mimic the behaviour of a neuron or a collection of neurons, i.e. computational devices which are distinguished by their hardware capability to modulate the weights and numbers of the interconnections of a multiplicity of computational components based on previous data". The 2025 update of Annex I (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/2003) kept the entry.

### Slide 5 — Transaction-related risks (Student A)

**On the slide**

| Risk | What can go wrong here | How we reduce it |
|---|---|---|
| **Product** | Licence refused or late. Goods diverted to a military user. | Licence as a condition of the contract. End-user statement, no-re-export clause. |
| **Country** | Mongolia lies between Russia and China and signed an interim trade agreement with the Eurasian Economic Union in 2025. OECD country risk 6 of 7. | Screening of the buyer. Audit right. Export credit insurance. |
| **Counterparty** | Buyer receives the goods, then does not pay the note. | Note in hand before shipment. Eksfin guarantee. |
| **Transport** | Loss or damage in the air or at the Istanbul transfer. Carrier liability is limited by weight. | Cargo insurance for 110 % of the value. |
| **Legal and documentary** | Note with a formal defect is not a valid note. Missing licence copy stops the goods at customs. | Note drafted by the bank. Document checklist. |
| **Currency** | Buyer earns tugrik, owes euros. Seller's costs are in kroner. | Invoice in euros. Forward cover on both sides. |

**Say (45 s):** "We see six groups of risk. The product risk is the licence and diversion. The country risk is geography: Mongolia's only neighbours are Russia and China, and it has a new trade agreement with the Eurasian Economic Union, so re-export to Russia is the scenario the Norwegian authority will worry about. The counterparty risk is the biggest commercial one, and my colleague comes back to it. The transport risk has one surprising number: under the Montreal Convention the airline's liability is limited by weight, here less than twenty thousand euros for a shipment worth 576,000. So insurance is not optional."

**Backup for questions:** ratings at the time of writing — S&P BB- (October 2025), Moody's B1 (October 2025). The carrier limit is 26 SDR per kg × 480 kg ≈ 12,480 SDR.

### Slide 6 — The intermediary: a commercial agent in Ulaanbaatar (Student A)

**On the slide**

**Blue Sky Trade Agency LLC** acts in the name and on behalf of NordNeuro. It finds and prepares the deal, does not buy the goods, and earns 5 % commission once the note is paid.

| | Commercial agent (our choice) | Distributor | Export management company |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who contracts with the end user | The exporter | The distributor | The exporter, through the EMC |
| Who bears the credit risk | The exporter | The distributor | The exporter |
| End user visible to the licensing authority | Yes, directly | One step removed | Yes |
| Price control | Exporter | Lost | Exporter |

- **Benefits:** local language and contacts · direct contract and direct note from the end user · help with import clearance, the end-user statement and chasing the note · cheaper than a distributor's margin.
- **Risks:** exporter keeps the credit risk · agent may exceed its authority · corruption exposure · compensation claims when the agency ends.
- **Our answers:** written agency agreement with no authority to sign or to receive money · anti-bribery clause · commission paid only on cash received.

**Say (45 s):** "The syllabus lets us choose between an agent, a distributor and an export management company. We chose a commercial agent in Ulaanbaatar. The reason is the nature of the goods. With a dual-use item the Norwegian authority wants to see exactly who the end user is. An agent only introduces the parties, so the contract and the promissory note stay directly between NordNeuro and Tengri. A distributor would buy and resell, and the end user would be one step away from us. The price of this choice is that the seller keeps the credit risk, so we pay the agent only when the note is paid."

### Slide 7 — CPT Incoterms® 2020: two places, two different moments (Student A)

**On the slide**

| | Place | What passes there |
|---|---|---|
| **Place of delivery** | NordNeuro dispatch dock, Instituttveien 18, 2007 Kjeller, Norway | **Risk** passes to the buyer when the goods are handed to the first carrier |
| **Named place of destination** | Chinggis Khaan International Airport (UBN), Cargo Terminal, Sergelen soum, Töv Province, Mongolia | Seller's **cost** obligation ends |

| Seller | Buyer |
|---|---|
| Export licence and export clearance | Import clearance, duty, VAT |
| Contracts and pays carriage to Ulaanbaatar airport | Bears the risk of the whole flight |
| Hands the goods to the carrier in Kjeller | Unloading and terminal charges at destination, unless the carriage contract includes them |
| Sends the air waybill and notice of dispatch | On-carriage airport to city, 52 km |
| No duty to insure | No duty to insure, but every reason to |

**Say (45 s):** "CPT means Carriage Paid To. It has two critical points, and they are more than five thousand kilometres apart. Risk passes in Kjeller, the moment the seller hands the cartons to the first carrier. Cost passes at Ulaanbaatar airport, because the seller pays the freight that far. So the buyer carries the risk of a journey that the seller organised and paid for. And CPT obliges neither party to insure. That gap is the main weakness of this term, and our recommendation is that the buyer takes out cargo insurance from door to door."

**Backup for questions:** CPT suits any mode of transport, including air. If the parties want the seller to insure, the matching term is CIP, where Incoterms 2020 requires all-risks cover.

**Say (35 s):** "Look at where the documents go. The invoice, the packing list and the air waybill go straight to the buyer. Only one paper travels through the banks: the promissory note. And an air waybill is not a document of title like a bill of lading. The airline hands the goods to the consignee named on it. So the seller has nothing in its hand that the buyer needs in order to get the goods. This is what makes the payment method risky."

### Slide 10 — The payment method: promissory note with clean collection (Student B)

**On the slide**

**Promissory note:** a written, unconditional promise by the maker (Tengri) to pay a fixed sum to the payee (NordNeuro) on a fixed day. Two parties. Independent of the sales contract. Transferable by endorsement.

**Clean collection (URC 522):** the banks collect a financial document that is not accompanied by commercial documents. They act as agents. They do not promise payment.

| Step | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tengri (maker) | Issues the note for EUR 576,000, payable 22 Feb 2027 at Khan Bank; sends it to NordNeuro before shipment |
| 2 | NordNeuro (principal) | Ships the goods; sends the commercial documents directly to Tengri |
| 3 | NordNeuro | Gives the note and a collection instruction to DNB (remitting bank) |
| 4 | DNB | Forwards both to Khan Bank (collecting and presenting bank) |
| 5 | Khan Bank | Presents the note to Tengri at maturity |
| 6 | Tengri | Pays → Khan Bank remits to DNB → NordNeuro is credited |
| 6b | If not paid | Khan Bank advises non-payment and protests the note if instructed |

**Balance of risk:** seller high — buyer low.

**Say (50 s):** "A promissory note is the buyer's own written promise to pay 576,000 euros on a fixed day. It is unconditional and independent of the sales contract. Clean collection means the seller's bank, DNB, sends only this note to Khan Bank in Ulaanbaatar, and Khan Bank presents it for payment at maturity. Under the ICC rules, URC 522, the banks are only messengers. They check nothing and guarantee nothing. So in substance this is an open-account sale with a ninety-day credit, plus a piece of paper that makes it easier to sue or to sell the debt. It is cheap and fast. It is very good for the buyer and weak for the seller."

**Backup for questions:** a note needs the words "promissory note" in its text, an unconditional promise of a fixed sum, the maturity, the place of payment, the payee, the date and place of issue and the maker's signature. Under URC 522 a bank protests only if the collection instruction says so.

### Slide 11 — The seller: rights, duties and protection (Student B)

**On the slide**

| Duties | Rights |
|---|---|
| Deliver conforming goods and the invoice (CISG 30, 35) | Receive a valid note before shipment |
| Obtain the export licence and clear export | Payment at maturity, plus interest if late (CISG 62, 78) |
| Contract and pay carriage to Ulaanbaatar airport | Suspend delivery or stop the goods in transit if the buyer clearly will not pay (CISG 71) |
| Hand the goods to the carrier in the agreed period | Avoid the contract and claim damages (CISG 64, 74–76) |
| Send the air waybill and notice of dispatch | Sell or discount the note |
| Pack and mark for air transport | |

**Risk-mitigation tools**

1. No shipment before the signed note is in hand.
2. Supplier credit guarantee from Eksfin, Norway's export credit agency.
3. Discount or forfait the note, so the bank carries the waiting.
4. Retention of title in the contract.
5. Protest and court action on the note itself if it is dishonoured.
6. Licence as a condition precedent. No-re-export and end-use clauses.

**Instruction to the carrier if the buyer becomes insolvent while the goods are in the air:**
"As shipper under air waybill 235-1234 5675 we exercise our right of disposition. Do not deliver to the consignee. Hold the consignment at our disposal and await return instructions."

**Say (50 s):** "The seller's duties under CPT are to get the export licence, to book and pay the flight, to hand over conforming goods on time, and to send the air waybill and a notice of dispatch. Its main right is to be paid at maturity. Because the note is unsecured, the seller needs its own protection. First, it does not ship until it holds the signed note. Second, it buys a supplier credit guarantee from Eksfin. Third, it can sell the note to a bank. And if the buyer goes bankrupt while the goods are still flying, the shipper can order the airline not to deliver. That right ends when the buyer collects the goods."

### Slide 12 — The buyer: rights, duties and protection (Student B)

**On the slide**

| Duties | Rights |
|---|---|
| Issue a valid note and pay it at maturity (CISG 53, 54) | Conforming goods, on time (CISG 35) |
| Give a truthful end-user statement and the data for the licence | The air waybill and notice of dispatch, in time to insure and collect |
| Take over the goods from the carrier at Ulaanbaatar (CISG 60) | Repair or replacement, price reduction (CISG 46, 50) |
| Clear import, pay VAT and local charges | Extra time, then avoidance of the contract (CISG 47, 49) |
| Bear the transport risk from Kjeller | Damages, including the cost of a cover purchase (CISG 74–76) |
| Examine the goods and report defects quickly (CISG 38, 39) | Claim against the carrier for loss or damage |
| No re-export, civil use only | |

**Risk-mitigation tools**

1. Cargo insurance, all risks, door to door, for 110 % of the CPT value = EUR 633,600.
2. Factory acceptance test in Kjeller before signing the note.
3. Fixed delivery date with a penalty for delay and a final date for the licence.
4. Warranty period and a retained right to claim on the contract after paying.
5. Euro forward contract against the tugrik.

**Instructions:** to the insurer — cover from the seller's door to the lab · to the carrier — written notice of damage within 14 days of receipt.

**Say (50 s):** "The buyer's first duty is to give the note and to pay it. The second is honesty about the end use, because the licence depends on it. Then it must clear import and collect the goods. Its biggest risk is not payment but transport, since under CPT it owns the risk from Kjeller. So it insures for 110 percent of the value. There is a second, less obvious risk. A promissory note is unconditional. If the computers arrive defective, the buyer must still pay the note and then claim under the contract. That is why we advise a factory acceptance test before the note is signed."

### Slide 13 — Possible breaches and the remedies (Student B)

**On the slide**

| Breach | By | Remedy or prevention |
|---|---|---|
| No export licence → late or no delivery | Seller | Extra time, then avoidance; final date in the contract (CISG 47, 49) |
| Defective or wrong goods | Seller | Notice in time, repair or replacement, price reduction, damages |
| No notice of dispatch → buyer cannot insure | Seller | Damages; insure under an open policy from the contract date |
| Note not issued, or formally invalid | Buyer | Seller withholds shipment (CISG 71) |
| False end-user statement, re-export to Russia | Buyer | Contract ends, penalty, report to DEKSA; licence can be withdrawn |
| Goods not collected at Ulaanbaatar | Buyer | Storage at buyer's cost; seller may resell (CISG 85, 88) |
| Note not paid, paid late or in part | Buyer | Protest, action on the note, interest, Eksfin claim |
| Loss, damage or delay in transit | Carrier | Buyer claims on insurance; insurer recovers from the carrier |

**Say (40 s):** "Here is what can go wrong and who answers for it. On the seller's side the typical breach is delay, because the licence takes longer than planned. On the buyer's side there are two serious ones. The first is non-payment of the note, answered by protest, court action and the Eksfin guarantee. The second is specific to dual-use goods: a false end-user statement or a re-export. That is not only a breach of contract, it is a breach of export control law, and the seller is exposed too. This is why the compliance clauses matter as much as the payment clauses."

### Slide 14 — Our critical assessment: what we would renegotiate (Student B)

**On the slide**

1. **The payment term is too weak for this deal.** A first sale to a buyer in a 6-of-7 risk country on an unsecured note is an open-account sale in disguise. We would ask for a **bank aval** on the note from Khan Bank, or a 20–30 % advance payment.
2. **CPT leaves the cargo uninsured by default.** We would move to **CIP**, where the seller must buy all-risks cover. The seller already controls the route, which is right for a licensed item.
3. **Compliance is a commercial term here.** Licence as a condition precedent, no-re-export clause, audit right. Route with no stop in Russia.
4. **The agent should share the payment risk.** A del credere commission would give the agent a real interest in the buyer's solvency.
5. **Open question for research:** entry 4A004.b has been on the control list for decades. Does it still describe the hardware that matters, now that AI runs mostly on conventional accelerator chips?

**Say (40 s):** "Our conclusion is that the given terms work, but they are unbalanced. The seller gives ninety days of credit to a new buyer in a high-risk country, with no bank behind the note. We would ask Khan Bank to add its aval, which turns company risk into bank risk. We would also change CPT to CIP so that the cargo is insured by contract and not by good advice. And we leave one open question: the control entry for neural computers has been on the list for decades, while most AI today runs on ordinary accelerator chips. Whether the list still controls the right hardware would be worth researching. Thank you."
